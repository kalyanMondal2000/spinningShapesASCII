Mathematical Principles & Code Integration

1. 3D Rotation Matrices

To rotate points in 3D space around origin coordinates, trigonometric rotation matrices are applied for X, Y, and Z axes. For example, rotating around the X-axis:

x' = x
y' = y·cos(θ) - z·sin(θ)
z' = y·sin(θ) + z·cos(θ)

2. Perspective Projection

To map 3D coordinates to a 2D text cell grid, a perspective division formula scales points inversely relative to depth z:

projX = ((x * aspect) * fov / z) * (cols / 2) + (cols / 2)

3. Rasterization & Flat Shading

Polygons are split into triangles and rasterized using scanlines with a Z-buffer to hide occluded pixels. Surface normals computed via cross products are dot-multiplied against directional light to determine shade density characters from ".,-~:;=!?*#$@".
